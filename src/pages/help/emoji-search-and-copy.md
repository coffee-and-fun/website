## Search for one

Open [Emoji Search & Copy](/emoji/) and start typing.

Search matches emoji names and keywords, so you don't need the official name. Type `happy`, `party`, `money`, `sad` and you'll get sensible results without knowing that 🎉 is formally a "party popper".

## Copy it

Click any emoji to copy it. Paste it wherever you need with <kbd class="kbd kbd-sm">⌘</kbd> + <kbd class="kbd kbd-sm">V</kbd> or <kbd class="kbd kbd-sm">Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd class="kbd kbd-sm">V</kbd>.

## Browse by category

If you don't know what you're looking for, browse the categories instead. Faster than searching when you want to see the options rather than find one specific thing.

There's also a most-copied list, which is a reasonable shortcut to the ones people actually use.

## Recently used

Emoji you've copied are kept in a recently used list, so the ones you reach for repeatedly stay at hand.

This lives in your browser on this device. It isn't an account, so it won't follow you to another computer.

## Why an emoji looks different elsewhere

The single most common surprise: you copy an emoji, send it, and it looks different on the recipient's phone.

That's expected. You're sending a character, not a picture, and every platform draws its own. Apple, Google, Microsoft and Samsung all have their own artwork for the same character. Some differ enough to change the tone of a message. We wrote about [why the same emoji looks different on every phone](/blog/why-emoji-look-different-on-every-phone/) if you want the full story.

Practical version: **don't rely on a specific emoji's appearance to carry meaning** in anything important.

## If an emoji shows as a box

A box or a question mark means the device doesn't have artwork for that character, usually because the emoji is newer than the operating system. Nothing is broken and the character is intact; the recipient's system just can't draw it. Older emoji are safer for wide audiences.
