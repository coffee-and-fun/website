## Step 1: Build and copy it

Open the [Email Signature Generator](/email-signature-generator/), fill in your details, pick a template, and copy the signature. The generator has an Apple Mail tab in its setup panel.

> **Paste, don't retype.** Signature editors accept formatted content pasted in. Typing it out by hand loses the layout, the links and the spacing.

## Step 2: Open signature settings on Mac

In Mail, go to **Mail → Settings → Signatures**.

Pick the account in the left column first. If you select **All Signatures** instead, the signature isn't attached to any account and won't appear automatically.

Click **+** to create one and give it a name.

## Step 3: Turn off the plain text option

This is the step that catches everyone.

Underneath the signature editing area there's a checkbox: **Always match my default message font**. If it's ticked, Mail strips your formatting and shows plain text.

**Untick it before pasting.** If you've already pasted and it looks plain, untick the box, delete what's there, and paste again.

## Step 4: Paste and assign it

Paste into the right-hand editing pane.

Then, at the bottom of the window, use **Choose Signature** to set the default for that account. If you have several signatures, you can also pick **At Random** or **In Sequential Order**, which is rarely what anyone wants.

## iPhone and iPad

Go to **Settings → Apps → Mail → Signature**.

This field is plain text only. You can't paste a formatted signature into it and there's no workaround inside the Mail app.

Set something short here, and keep the full version on your Mac.

> **A note on the default:** iOS ships with "Sent from my iPhone" and most people never change it. Two minutes here is the highest-return signature edit available to anyone.

## Troubleshooting

**It's plain text no matter what.** The **Always match my default message font** box is still ticked, or it was ticked when you pasted. Untick, clear, paste again.

**The signature isn't attached to messages.** You created it under **All Signatures** without assigning it to an account, or you didn't set it in **Choose Signature**.

**The photo doesn't load for recipients.** It needs a public URL, and many mail clients block remote images by default. Never put your only contact details inside an image.
