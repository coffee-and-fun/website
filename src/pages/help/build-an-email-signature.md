## Step 1: Fill in your details

Open the [Email Signature Generator](/email-signature-generator/) and start typing. The preview updates as you go.

The fields available:

- **Name, job title, company**
- **Email**, and a **secondary email** if you use one
- **Phone** and **website**
- **Calendar link**, for booking pages
- **Photo URL**, which must be a publicly reachable image

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded and there's no account.

## Step 2: Pick a template

Six layouts, all built to render in real mail clients rather than only in a browser:

**Classic**, **Modern**, **Corporate**, **Creative**, **Elegant**, **Tech**.

They're table-based on purpose. That's the only layout approach Outlook renders predictably, and a signature that breaks in Outlook breaks for a large share of business email.

## Step 3: Add social links

Add whichever you actually use. Available: LinkedIn, Twitter, GitHub, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, Telegram, WhatsApp, Medium, Dribbble.

Any field left blank is left out of the signature entirely, so an unused network costs you nothing.

> **Fewer is better.** Every link is a decision you're asking the reader to make. Two or three that matter beat nine that don't.

## Step 4: Add a photo, or don't

If you use one, the URL has to be public. An image on your own computer, or behind a login, will show as broken to everyone receiving it.

Two things to remember about images in email:

- Many clients block remote images until the recipient allows them, so **never put contact details only inside an image**.
- The image loads from wherever you host it, every time someone opens the email.

## Step 5: Copy it

Two copy options:

**Copy the signature** gives you the formatted version. This is the one you want in almost every case. Paste it straight into your mail client's signature editor.

**Copy HTML code** gives you the raw markup, for cases where a system asks for HTML source: some CRMs, help desks, and company-wide signature tools.

Then follow the guide for your client: [Gmail](/help/add-email-signature-in-gmail/), [Outlook](/help/add-email-signature-in-outlook/), or [Apple Mail](/help/add-email-signature-in-apple-mail/).

## What makes a signature work

The mechanics are easy; the judgement is the hard part. Briefly:

**Keep it under about five lines.** It appears on every message, including one-word replies.

**Don't repeat what the email header already says.** Your name and address are visible above the message.

**Skip the legal disclaimer** unless your industry requires one. They're long, nobody reads them, and their legal weight is doubtful.

**Test it before rolling it out.** Send to yourself and to one colleague on a different mail client.
