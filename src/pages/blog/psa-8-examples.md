PSA 8 is where card grading stops being a magnification exercise.

At 10 and 9 you are hunting for defects with tools. At 8 you can find them by holding the card and looking at it properly. That shift is the most useful thing to understand about the grade, because it means **you can pre-screen an 8 yourself, for free, in about thirty seconds.**

I own a PSA 8, bought alongside a 10 and a 9 for [the grading estimator project](/blog/how-psa-grades-pokemon-cards-research-guide/). Its flaws are very slight rounding on two corners, barely visible without a loupe but visible with one.

## What puts a card at 8

Either more than one findable flaw, or one flaw that is clearly visible without magnification:

- Light corner wear on more than one corner
- Whitening visible along an edge at normal reading distance
- Centering noticeably off, in the region of 65/35 on the front
- Surface scratching that shows when you tilt the card under a light
- Loss of some original gloss

The card still looks good. Nobody picking it up would call it damaged. It just no longer passes the "I cannot find anything wrong" test.

## How to spot an 8 before you pay

Do this before spending [$79.99 a card](https://www.psacard.com/services/tradingcardgrading):

**Tilt it under a single light source.** Not overhead room light. One lamp, and rotate the card slowly. Scratches and gloss loss appear at specific angles and are invisible at every other angle.

**Look at all four corners at reading distance.** No loupe. If you can see any of them are less sharp than the others, you are at 8 or below.

**Run your eye along each edge against a dark background.** Edge whitening on a dark-bordered card is obvious against black and nearly invisible against a white desk.

**Measure the centering.** If it is past about 60/40 on the front you are almost certainly not getting a 9. [Here is how to measure it properly](/blog/psa-centering-tolerances/).

If the card passes all four, submit it. If it fails one, you are looking at an 8, and the question becomes whether an 8 of that card is worth eighty dollars plus two months. Usually it is not, unless the card is genuinely valuable.

## The grade where grading often stops making sense

A PSA 8 is a perfectly respectable grade for a vintage card and a fairly ordinary one for a modern card, and that difference is the whole economics.

For a 1999 card, an 8 can be a strong result, because very few survived better and the market prices that in. For a card printed last year, an 8 means the card is worse than most copies in existence, and the graded price will reflect that.

Same grade, opposite meaning. PSA say as much themselves when they describe judging against what the market accepts for that particular issue. Before you submit anything modern, check what an 8 of that card actually sells for.

## Where this page is incomplete

One card, one set. Two slightly rounded corners is one route to an 8 out of at least five listed above, and my card does not demonstrate the others.
