The single most common thing people search about card grading is not how grading works. It is what a grade looks like.

Forty three different queries reach this site asking to *see* a grade. "psa 7 example", "what does a psa 8 look like", "psa 1-10 examples". Together they are 14,512 impressions a year at an average position of 8.7. Every one of them lands on a post made of words.

Words are the wrong format for this question. You cannot describe a corner. So this page is the index, each grade gets its own page with photographs, and I am going to be straight with you about which photographs exist yet.

## The scale, in one table

| Grade | Name | The short version |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | Gem Mint | Nothing wrong that anyone can find |
| **9** | Mint | One tiny thing, usually centering or a single soft corner |
| **8** | NM-MT | Minor wear you need to look for |
| **7** | NM | Light wear you can see without looking for it |
| **6** | EX-MT | Obvious handling, possibly a light crease |
| **5** | EX | Rounded corners, real edge wear |
| **4** | VG-EX | Multiple flaws, creasing |
| **3** | VG | Heavy creases, rounding, clear damage |
| **2** | Good | Significant damage, card intact |
| **1** | Poor | Severe damage, tears, missing pieces |

## The thing that surprises everyone

The gap between a 10 and a 7 is smaller than you think, and the gap between a 7 and a 4 is enormous.

Grades 10 through 8 are decided by defects you often need magnification and raking light to find. PSA's own Joe Orlando wrote about [corner flips](https://www.psacard.com/articles/articleview/5327/taking-my-hacks-few-more-grading-tips) being "responsible for more otherwise NM-MT or Mint cards dropping to a grade of PSA NM 7", and a corner flip is a defect you can hold in your hand and never notice.

From 6 downward the flaws are obvious across a room. Creases, rounded corners, missing surface.

This matters practically. If you are looking at a card and can immediately see what is wrong with it, you are not in a 10 versus 9 conversation. You are somewhere at 6 or below, and the expensive grading tiers make no sense.

## What separates each grade from the one above

This is the part that actually answers the question, so here it is in one place.

**10 to 9.** One thing. Centering slightly past tolerance, or one corner that is very slightly soft under magnification, or a single faint surface line. A 9 is not a flawed card. It is a card with exactly one findable thing.

**9 to 8.** The flaw stops needing magnification. A whitened corner tip you can see at reading distance, or centering that is visibly off, or a scratch you catch when you tilt the card.

**8 to 7.** More than one flaw, or one flaw that is clearly visible. Light corner fraying on multiple corners, edge whitening along one side, surface scratching that shows in normal light.

**7 to 6.** Something structural. A light crease, a dent, a corner that has gone from worn to rounded. This is where the card stops looking like a good copy and starts looking like a used one.

**6 to 5 and below.** Accumulation. Multiple creases, all four corners rounded, surface loss, edge damage on every side. Below a 4 the card is being graded on whether it is complete rather than how it looks.

## What I can and cannot show you

Here is the honest inventory.

I own three graded cards, bought specifically to study for [the AI grading estimator project](/blog/how-psa-grades-pokemon-cards-research-guide/): a PSA 10, a PSA 9, and a PSA 8. Those three I can photograph properly, at the same magnification, under the same light, which is the only way a comparison means anything.

I do not own a PSA 7, and I do not own anything below it.

That leaves me three options, and I am telling you which one I picked. I could photograph nothing and describe grades in prose, which is what every guide already does and what is failing these searches. I could use images from PSA's own listings or auction sites, which I do not have the rights to reproduce. Or I could show you the three cards I actually have and be explicit about the gap.

I picked the third. The PSA 7 page will say it is waiting on a card until I own one.

## Why "example" searches go unanswered

A thought about why this gap exists at all.

Photographing graded cards well is genuinely hard. The slab is reflective, the defects are sub-millimetre, and raking light that reveals a scratch also blows out the holo. Most guides skip the photos because photos are work and prose is not.

The result is that the highest-volume question in the hobby is answered almost entirely by people who have never held the grade they are describing. Including, until I shoot these three cards, me.

That is the argument for doing it properly rather than quickly. There is no shortage of pages describing a PSA 7. There is a shortage of pages showing one.
