## What a PSA 7 is

Near Mint. Light wear that you can see without looking for it, on a card that is still clearly in good condition.

In practice, one or more of:

- Light fraying or fuzz on several corners
- Whitening along one or more edges, visible at a glance
- Surface scratching that shows in ordinary light
- Centering around 70/30 on the front, which reads as visibly off centre
- Minor loss of gloss across the card

The threshold that matters: at a 7, someone who does not collect cards would still say the card looks fine. At a 6, they would start to notice something is wrong with it.

## The corner flip, which lives here

PSA's own Joe Orlando wrote in 2008 that corner flips are ["responsible for more otherwise NM-MT or Mint cards dropping to a grade of PSA NM 7"](https://www.psacard.com/articles/articleview/5327/taking-my-hacks-few-more-grading-tips).

A corner flip is a tiny fold at the very tip of a corner, often invisible straight on and obvious under raking light. It is worth knowing about specifically because it is the defect most likely to turn a card you were confident about into a 7. If you are pre-screening cards, tilt them and look at the corner tips against the light.

## Should you grade a 7

Usually not, and the arithmetic is simple.

At [$79.99 for Regular service](https://www.psacard.com/services/tradingcardgrading), a PSA 7 needs to be worth meaningfully more than the raw card plus eighty dollars to justify the submission. For most modern cards it is not close. For genuinely scarce vintage, where a 7 might be near the top of the surviving population, it can be.

Work out what a 7 of your specific card sells for before you decide. Not what a 10 sells for. A 7.
